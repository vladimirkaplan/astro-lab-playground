Lab 10 · Series and parallel resistor rules

Drag a 100 ohm and a 200 ohm resistor onto the breadboard. Make a series pair, then a parallel pair, at a fixed 6.0 V supply. Measure the total voltage and current, and test the combination rules with the meter values.

Words in this lab

  • series circuit — one loop. The same current runs through every part.
  • parallel circuit — two branches between the same two nodes. The branches split the current.
  • node — a marked point on the wire where a voltmeter probe can sit. The letters name them.
  • ammeter — the current meter, in mA. It wires in series, in the line of the current.
  • voltmeter — the voltage meter, in V. Its two probes go across two nodes.
  • total resistance — what the supply sees. Each layout has its own rule for it.
  • watt (W) — the unit of power. 1 W = 1 V times 1 A.
  • enable switch — the switch that puts power on the fixed 6.0 V supply.

What you do at the bench

The bench has a DC supply fixed at 6.0 V. The manual sets one set-point, so the supply has an enable switch and no knob.

You get two resistors: 100 Ω and 200 Ω, nominal values printed on the body.

An ammeter on the 100 mA scale sits in the main branch. A voltmeter on the 15 V range carries two probes.

In Part 1 you drag the two resistors onto the breadboard and pass the quick instructor check before any power goes on.

In Part 2 the pair is in series. Close the tap key, read the meters, and log Table A.

In Part 3 you drag the resistors onto the two branches. The pair is in parallel, and you log Table B.

A tap key closes the circuit while you read the meters.

The one equation

Each layout has a rule for the total resistance, and Ohm’s law takes the rest:

  • Series: R_total = R1 + R2. The current is the same everywhere: I0 = I1 = I2. The drops add: V0 = V1 + V2.
  • Parallel: 1/R_total = 1/R1 + 1/R2. The voltage is the same on both branches: V0 = V1 = V2. The currents add: I0 = I1 + I2.

Measure the resistance from the meters: R_measured = V0 / I0.

Power from the total: P = V0·I0 = I0²·R_total.

How the meters sit

The ammeter reads the main current I0 by default. A teaching mode moves it into branch 1 or branch 2.

In a series pair it reads I0 anywhere, because the current is the same everywhere in one loop.

In a parallel pair the branches differ: the main branch carries I0, and each branch carries its own share.

The voltmeter touches two nodes with its probes and reads the drop between them.

Series nodes are A, B, C in a line. Parallel nodes are A and C on the top rail, B and D on the bottom rail.

The honest limits

The parallel total current is 90 mA. That sits close to the 100 mA stop on the ammeter, and the bench keeps the margin in view.

Add a third resistor in parallel and the meter pegs. That is the meter range showing up, not new physics.

Real meters have internal resistance. A node voltage can sit tens of millivolts off the ideal value.

The nominal resistors carry a 5 percent tolerance band. The manual does not print the band, so the bench names it here.

That means a percent error near zero is a warning, not a great result.

Each part is one run. A real lab repeats every part, and the scatter between runs is part of the data.

Common mistakes in this lab:
  • Leaving the ammeter in a branch and logging that value as I0 in parallel.
  • Turning the power on before the instructor check has passed.
  • Expecting the two parallel branches to carry the same current.
  • Adding V1 and V2 in parallel and expecting the sum to equal V0.
  • Writing 20 mA as 0.020 in the milliamp column.

Part 1 — Place the resistors and pass the check

  1. Drag the 100 Ω chip and the 200 Ω chip out of the tray.
  2. Drop both chips on the series slots R1 and R2, one per slot.
  3. Press "Instructor check". The bench names the layout and anything missing.
  4. The check must pass before the enable switch will do anything.
  5. Drag a chip back to the tray to start over. The power drops when the layout changes.

Part 2 — The series pair

  1. With both chips in the series slots, press the enable switch, then close the tap key.
  2. Read the ammeter in mA. In a series pair it reads I0 in every position.
  3. Drag the voltmeter probes onto A and B to read V1, then B and C for V2, then A and C for V0.
  4. Press "Log Table A". The one-shot table fills from the meter values.
  5. Open the key before you touch the chips again. The bench drops power on rewire.

Part 3 — The parallel pair

  1. Drag both chips from the series slots to the branch slots P1 and P2.
  2. Press "Instructor check" again, then the enable switch, then the tap key.
  3. Read I0 on the main branch. Switch the ammeter to branch 1 and branch 2 to read I1 and I2.
  4. Drag the probes across A and B for V1, and across C and D for V2. Both equal V0 here.
  5. Press "Log Table B". The branch currents carry their own sum check.
  6. Optional: press "Borrow third 100 Ω". The total current passes the 100 mA stop and the bench names it.

Drag this — chips go tray to slot and back. Probes go tray to node and back. Click a placed chip or probe and drag it to move it.
