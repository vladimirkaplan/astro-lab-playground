Lab 11 · Speed of sound from a resonance tube

Drag the water cup in a vertical tube and listen for two loud spots. The space between them is half a wavelength. Work it for two tuning forks, and compare your speed with the printed temperature law.

Words in this lab

  • resonance — the water level where the air column rings loudest.
  • air column — the stretch of air between the water surface and the tube mouth.
  • node — the spot in a standing wave where the air stays still.
  • antinode — the spot where the air moves the most.
  • tuning fork — a metal fork that rings at one fixed frequency, in hertz.
  • hertz (Hz) — cycles per second. The number stamped on the fork is its frequency.
  • open-end correction — a small extra length the open mouth adds. It shifts every resonance by the same amount.
  • temperature law — the printed formula v_T = 331.5 + 0.6·T, in m/s with T in °C.

What you do at the bench

The bench has a vertical tube in a water reservoir. A cm scale runs down the tube, zero at the mouth.

Drag the cup up and down to set the water level, and so the air column length L.

The fork rack holds two forks: 512 Hz and 440 Hz. Click a fork to sound it above the tube mouth.

Loudness swells at the mouth as you pass a resonance. The maximum is broad, so move the cup slowly and watch for the top of the swell.

Log L1 at the first loud spot and L2 at the next one, for each fork.

Below the table the bench averages your two speeds and compares them with the temperature law at the room temperature.

A room thermometer sets T_air for the accepted value.

The one equation

The wave law carries the lab:

  • v = f·λ

A closed-open tube resonates at the odd quarter wavelengths: L near λ/4, then near 3λ/4, each with a small open-end correction.

The spacing between two successive resonances is one half wavelength:

  • λ = 2·(L2 − L1)

The accepted speed comes from the printed law:

  • v_T = 331.5 + 0.6·T_air (m/s, T in °C)

Why the difference works

The first resonance sits at λ/4 plus the end correction.

The second sits at 3λ/4 plus the same correction.

Subtract the two: the correction cancels, and L2 − L1 is exactly λ/2.

That makes the spacing a cleaner ruler for the wavelength than the first position alone.

At the first resonance the node is at the closed end, the water surface, and the antinode is at the open mouth.

The honest limits

The loudness maximum is broad. A realistic position uncertainty is ±1 to 2 cm around the top of the swell.

Real tubes have a diameter. The end correction is about 0.3 times the diameter, and this bench assumes a 4 cm tube.

Forks drop in pitch a little when you hold them, and makers stamp a tolerance on the hertz.

Read the water level straight on. A slanted eye, the meniscus, or a ruler zero that misses the mouth shifts every reading.

A real lab has background noise that can mask the maximum. This bench shows a clean view and says it is idealized.

The air is treated as dry. The manual does not specify a humidity correction, so the bench ignores it and says so.

Common mistakes in this lab:
  • Stopping at the first resonance. The spacing needs both.
  • Using the first position alone for the wavelength.
  • Writing the level in centimeters where the table wants meters.
  • Comparing your speed with a memorized 343 m/s instead of the printed law at the room temperature.
  • Logging L2 above L1 and taking a negative spacing.

Part 1 — Sound a fork and find the first resonance

  1. Click the 512 Hz fork. It sounds above the tube mouth.
  2. Drag the cup down until the loudness bar swells. The first swell tops out near 18 cm.
  3. Move the cup up and down a centimeter or two, and keep the loudest level.
  4. Press "Log L1". The row for this fork takes the level in cm and m.
  5. The maximum is broad. A level within a couple of centimeters of the top is an honest find.

Part 2 — Find the second resonance

  1. Keep the same fork sounding, and drag the cup down past the first swell.
  2. The second swell tops out near 52 cm for the 512 Hz fork.
  3. Press "Log L2". The bench works the spacing, the wavelength, and the speed for this fork.
  4. The table fills the f, L1, L2, lambda and v columns for the row.
  5. Only the spacing sets the wavelength. The end correction cancels in it.

Part 3 — Repeat with the other fork

  1. Click the 440 Hz fork and sound it above the mouth.
  2. Find and log L1, near 21 cm, then L2, near 60 cm.
  3. The spacing is wider for the lower fork, about 39 cm against 34 cm.
  4. When both rows are full, the summary line averages the two speeds.
  5. Read the average against the temperature-law value at 25 °C, and work the percent error.

Not printed in the manual, shown here so nothing is a guess:

The sim’s own setup. The manual’s standing-wave and apparatus figures are image-only. This tube, cup, fork rack and loudness bar are the sim’s own standard bench, with a deliberately broad loudness maximum and an idealized quiet room.

Fun Mode noise. Each logged level carries a stable ±0.2 cm ruler parallax. The loudness maximum stays broad, so the realistic ±1 to 2 cm find-uncertainty applies in both modes. Fun Mode never changes the temperature law, the room temperature, or the fork stamps.
