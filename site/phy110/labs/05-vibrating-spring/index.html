Lab 5 · Spring constant and vertical spring periods

Load a hanging spring and pull K out of your F–x data, then time ten cycles and compare your period with 2π√(m/K). The bench grades you against your own K, and your number is allowed to come out somewhere else.

Words in this lab

  • spring constant K — how stiff a spring is. Bigger K means harder to stretch. Its units are N/m.
  • elongation — how far the spring stretches below its no-load level. Written x, in metres or cm.
  • hanger — the little pan that hangs from the spring. Its mass counts as part of the load.
  • period T — the time for one full cycle, down and back.
  • frequency f — cycles per second. It is the flip of the period: f = 1/T.
  • x₀ — the level of the spring’s bottom end with nothing hanging, captured once.
  • mirror sight — a mirror strip behind the scale. You read the level when the mark lines up with its own image.

What you do at the bench

The bench has a vertical stand and a cm scale with a mirror strip behind it. A spring hangs from the top bar, with a weight hanger and slotted weights in 100, 50 and 20 g.

In Bench A you capture x₀ with the hanger off. Then hang the hanger and add the weights in 50 g steps, up to 250 g. Read the mark at every load. That fills the F–x table and the graph. K comes out two ways: row by row, and from the trendline slope.

In Bench B you hang 200, 220 and 240 g added, pull the hanger down and let go, and time ten cycles at each load. The period comes out of your ten-cycle times, and the bench compares it with the formula using your own K.

The one equation that matters

The hanging weight is the force, and it stretches the spring by x:

  • F = K·x

Turn it around and the stiffness is on its own: K = F/x. Then the oscillator side is one line of its own:

  • T = 2π√(m/K)

Course mode uses π = 3.14, as printed. In m you put the total hanging mass in kg, hanger included.

Reading x the honest way

The mark is the bottom end of the spring. With nothing on it, that level is x₀. Under a load it sits at xf, and the stretch is the difference between the two.

The data page does it in the figure style: x = 1.7 − 1.1 = 0.6 cm. Forget to subtract x₀ and every row is too long by the same amount, and every K comes out too small.

Reading the scale with the mirror

Read the scale from an angle and the number you see shifts, a little more at the far marks. The mirror strip stops that.

Slide your eye until the mark and its mirror image line up, one hiding the other in a straight line. When they sit on top of each other, your eye, the mark, and the image share one level, and the reading is the true one.

Why your measured T runs a little long

The period formula assumes the spring has no mass. A real coil does, and a third of that mass moves with the spring as it turns over.

So the measured period runs a few percent longer than the formula says. That is correct behaviour, not a failure. A few percent difference is what a real bench gives, and the bench here says so out loud.

Common mistakes in this lab:
  • Forgetting to subtract x₀ from the mark reading.
  • Mixing grams and kg in the period formula. m must be in kg.
  • Timing one cycle instead of ten. Your slip is ten times smaller against ten cycles.
  • Loading past about 300 g total. Real springs creep and go non-linear near their max load.
  • Reading the scale from an angle, without the mirror sight.

Bench A — Find K from the F–x data

  1. With the hanger off, press "Mark x0" to capture the no-load level of the spring's bottom end.
  2. Hang the hanger, watch the mark settle, and press "Read x_f". The hanger-only baseline row fills in.
  3. Drag 50 g weights onto the hanger, one at a time, up to 250 g added.
  4. At each load, wait for the spring to settle, then press "Read x_f". Every row gets F, x_f, x, x in m, and its own K.
  5. The graph plots F against x, with the (0,0) origin point always on the line.
  6. Drag the red handle to lay your trendline through the points. Its slope is your K from the trendline.
  7. The footer compares K_average with the trendline K, and gives the percent relative difference on the slope.

Bench B — Time the period

  1. Leave 200 g on the hanger, then 220 g, then 240 g. Those are the three period rows.
  2. Drag the hanger down a little and let go. The spring turns over.
  3. Press "Start 10-cycle timing". The counter marks each pass back through the middle.
  4. Count along. In Fun Mode press "Stop timing" at the tenth pass. In Ideal Mode the clock stops itself on the tenth pass.
  5. The bench divides by ten for T_measured and compares it with T_theory from your K_average.
  6. Repeat at the other two loads. The f cell reports 1/T at 200 g added.

Drag this — weights move between the tray and the hanger; the hanger itself moves down to start an oscillation, and let go to release it.
