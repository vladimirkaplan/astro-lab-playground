Lab 12 · Reflection, refraction, and the critical angle

Steer a laser at a flat water surface through a graduated tank. Check the reflection law angle by angle, log the refraction angles, and pull water’s index out of the measured critical angle.

Words in this lab

  • normal — the line at right angles to the surface, at the point where the beam lands.
  • angle of incidence — the angle between the incoming beam and the normal.
  • angle of reflection — the angle between the reflected beam and the normal.
  • angle of refraction — the angle between the bent beam and the normal, in the second medium.
  • index of refraction (n) — how much a medium slows light: n = c/v. Water is 1.333, air is 1.000.
  • critical angle — the incidence angle, from the dense side, at which the refracted beam just dies.
  • total internal reflection (TIR) — past the critical angle, all the light bounces back into the dense side.

What you do at the bench

The bench has a water tank with a circular graduated scale. The water line sits at the 90 mark, and the scale centre is the point where the beam lands.

A rotating laser box sits on the rim of the scale. Drag it around the circle, and its angle sets the incidence angle.

The box has two quadrant positions. Lower left, in the water, is for reflection and total internal reflection. Upper left, in the air, is for refraction.

In Part 1, laser in the water, you work the printed reflection set: 45° to 80° in 5° steps.

In Part 2, laser in the air, you work the printed refraction set: 10° to 80° in 10° steps.

In Part 3 you push the water-side angle past the critical angle and log the spot where the refracted beam dies.

A tap key switches the laser on and off. The eye-safety warning stays on the page at all times.

The one equation

All angles are measured from the normal. Two laws carry the lab:

  • θi = θr — the law of reflection.
  • n1·sin θ1 = n2·sin θ2 — Snell’s law.

The index of refraction is the slow-down factor:

  • n = c/v = λ0/λm

For the critical angle from water into air, with n_air = 1:

  • n = 1/sin θc

What changes at the boundary

Light slows in the water: v = c/n, about 2.25×10&sup8; m/s for n = 1.333.

The frequency does not change at the boundary.

The wavelength shortens in the denser medium: λm = λ0/n.

Into the denser medium the ray bends toward the normal; out of it, away.

The honest limits

The beam has width, so a scale reading sits about ±1° off the true angle.

Total internal reflection comes on gradually. The refracted beam dims near the critical angle instead of switching off.

A tilted surface or an off-centre laser pushes every reading off. Both are selectable error modes on this bench, so you can see the push.

1.333 is the 589 nm value. A red laser at 650 nm would see a slightly lower index. The manual keeps its single printed index, so the bench does too.

Common mistakes in this lab:
  • Measuring angles from the water surface instead of the normal.
  • Looking for TIR with the laser in the air. The dense medium must be on the incident side.
  • Logging an incidence angle that is not in the printed set.
  • Chasing the refracted beam after it has dimmed to nothing.

Part 1 — Reflection from the water side

  1. Pick "Laser in water". The box sits in the lower left quadrant, in the water.
  2. Close the tap key to turn the laser on. The warning line above stays put.
  3. Press "Next printed theta i" to step 45, 50, 55, ..., 80 degrees.
  4. Read the reflected angle on the scale, and compare it with the incidence angle.
  5. Press "Log pair" at each printed value. Table I fills row by row.
  6. Past about 49 degrees the refracted beam into the air dies, and the reflection stays at the equal angle.

Drag this — the laser box rides the scale rim. Drag it across the water line to switch quadrant. Eye safety: never look into the beam.

Part 2 — Refraction from the air side

  1. Pick "Laser in air". The box moves to the upper left quadrant.
  2. Turn the laser on if it dropped, and step the printed set: 10, 20, ..., 80 degrees.
  3. Read the refracted angle in the water against the scale.
  4. Press "Log pair" at each printed value. Table II fills row by row.
  5. Into the water the ray bends toward the normal, so the refracted angle is smaller.
  6. Below, the add-on graph plots sin θi against sin θ2. The slope of that line is the index n.

Part 3 — The critical angle

  1. Move the box back to the water side and keep the laser on.
  2. Drag the box past the printed set, toward the 90 line on the scale.
  3. Watch the air beam dim. The refracted beam dies at the critical angle.
  4. Press "Log critical angle" at the spot where the beam just goes out.
  5. The index box works n = 1/sin θc from your spot, against the printed 1.333.
