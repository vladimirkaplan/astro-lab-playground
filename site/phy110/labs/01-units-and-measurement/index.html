Lab 1 · Units, instruments, and percent error

Read four simple instruments, turn inches into centimetres and back, average five timings, and say how far off you are. This is the bench every other PHY110 lab stands on.

Words in this lab

  • measurement — a number plus the unit it came in.
  • unit — the thing you count with, like a centimetre.
  • SI units — the metric set: metre, gram, litre, newton.
  • British units — the older set: inch, foot, mile, pound.
  • conversion factor — a ratio equal to 1 that swaps units.
  • meniscus — the curved top of water in a glass tube.
  • average — add the readings, divide by how many.
  • percent error — how far your answer sits from the accepted one.

What you do at the bench

You get a shoebox, a metal object, a cylinder of water, a balance, a spring scale, a thermometer, a hallway, and a pendulum. For each one you read a number off a tool and write it down. Then you change the unit and do it again. The box does not change size. Your two answers should agree once the units are fixed.

That is the whole skill. Read the tool, keep the unit, and show the factor you used.

Every reading has two parts

A reading is a number and a unit. "12.7" means nothing. "12.7 cm" is a measurement. If you drop the unit, the number is wrong even when it is right.

You also read one digit nobody printed. The ruler has marks every millimetre, so you say "34.6 cm" and not "34 cm". The last digit is your best guess between two marks. It is part of the reading, so you never throw it away.

Trading one unit for another

The course prints its own factors. Use those, and write them on your line of work. The ones you will reach for:

  • 1 in = 2.54 cm, 1 yd = 0.91 m, 1 mi = 1.61 km.
  • 1 gal = 3.8 L, 1 lb = 0.45 kg, 1 kg = 2.2 lb.

A conversion factor is a fraction worth 1. You multiply by it, so the real size never changes. Only the name of the unit changes. Write the factor so the unit you want to lose sits underneath:

  • 66 in x (2.54 cm / 1 in) = 167.64 cm
  • 150 lb x (0.45 kg / 1 lb) = 67.5 kg

For volume the factor gets cubed. One cubic foot is 12 x 12 x 12 = 1728 cubic inches, not 12. This is the most common slip in the lab.

The equation that matters most

Percent error tells the instructor how close you got:

  • % error = |measured − accepted| / accepted x 100

Take the size of the gap, ignore the minus sign, divide by the accepted value, and multiply by 100. A 2% error in a first lab is a good day.

Two other lines do the rest of the work. Weight comes from mass with W = m x g, where g = 9.80 m/s² and mass goes in kg. Temperature crosses between scales with T(F) = 32 + (9/5) x T(C). The course also uses pi = 3.14 for hand arithmetic, so use 3.14 in this lab.

Why your answer comes out a little wrong

Real tools are coarse. The cylinder marks whole millilitres, so you read half of one by eye. Your paced step is never twice the same length. Your thumb lands late on the stopwatch. None of this is cheating; it is the lab.

Averaging is the fix. Time the pendulum five times and take the average, and each mistimed press matters a fifth as much. Then report the percent error, because a number without an error is just an opinion.

Common mistakes in this lab:
  • Writing a number with no unit on the line next to it.
  • Flipping the factor and getting 66 in = 26 cm.
  • Cubing the answer instead of the factor, or forgetting to cube at all.
  • Reading the top edge of the water instead of the bottom of the meniscus.
  • Calling the spring scale reading a mass. Newtons are weight, grams are mass.
  • Using the rounded 22.4 °C answer the manual shows for 72 °F. Plain 5/9 math gives 22.2 °C.

Bench A - Read the shoebox on both scales

  1. Pick a side: length, width, or height.
  2. Drag the ruler so its zero line sits on the box edge.
  3. Read the far edge in centimetres and millimetres.
  4. Flip the ruler to inches and read the same side again.
  5. Press "Log this side" and repeat for the other two sides.
  6. Press "Volume both ways" when all three sides are logged.

Drag this — the ruler slides left and right. If the zero line misses the box edge, both readings come out short by the same slip.

Bench B - Cylinder, balance, spring scale, thermometer

  1. Read the water level at the bottom of the meniscus.
  2. Press "Drop in the object" and read the new level.
  3. Subtract to get the object volume in mL.
  4. Weigh the object on the balance, then on the spring scale.
  5. Weigh the lab manual the same two ways.
  6. Toggle the thermometer to °F and read the water again.
  7. Log every reading before you leave this bench.

Bench C - Pace the hallway and the board

  1. Press "Step" once for each pace across the 50 ft course.
  2. Stop when the chalk mark says you have arrived.
  3. Read your step size in ft per step.
  4. Switch to the chalkboard span and pace it too.
  5. Multiply the board steps by your own step size.
  6. Log the course, the step size, and the board length.

Each press is one pace. Your pace length wobbles a little in both modes, because legs do that.

Bench D - Pendulum timing, five runs

  1. Set the string length near 0.90 m.
  2. Drag the bob aside and let go to start the swing.
  3. Press "Start clock" as the bob crosses the middle.
  4. Press "Stop clock" when the gate flashes 10.
  5. Do four more runs at the same length.
  6. Read your g and its percent error against 9.80.

Bench E - Build the conversion

  1. Type the value you want to change units on.
  2. Pick the unit you have and the unit you want.
  3. Press "Show the factors" to lay out the strip.
  4. Click the factors, one at a time, to drop them into the work line.
  5. Read the finished substitution, with the units cancelling.
  6. Press "Log this conversion" to keep it in your table.
