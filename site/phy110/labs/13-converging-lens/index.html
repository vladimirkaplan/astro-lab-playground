Lab 13 · Converging lens focal length and a two-lens refractor

Focus a lit arrow onto a screen at two lens positions, pull each focal length out of the lens equation, classify images at four printed distances, and space two lenses into a basic refractor.

Words in this lab

  • converging lens — a double-convex lens that bends parallel light to one point.
  • focal length (f) — the distance from the lens to that point, in metres.
  • object distance (o) — from the object to the lens, in metres.
  • image distance (i) — from the lens to the image, in metres.
  • real image — an image made of light that actually lands on a screen.
  • virtual image — an image you see only by looking through the lens. It never lands on a screen.
  • magnification (m) — the image-to-object size ratio, with the sign showing up or down.

What you do at the bench

The bench is 100 cm long with a 1 mm division scale.

The object is a 5 cm lit arrow on a post, with a light on and off.

Two double-convex lenses fit the holders. Lens A is blue, Lens B is amber.

Their focal lengths are printed on the lens rims in the drawer.

In Part 1 the arrow stands at the 0 mark and the white screen at the 100 mark, one metre apart.

You slide the lens until the arrow is sharp on the screen. That happens at two positions.

In Part 2 the lens sits fixed at the 50 mark. You place the arrow at four printed distances: 2.5f, 2.0f, 1.5f and 0.5f, and hunt for the image.

In Part 3 the two lenses form a basic refractor. The long-f lens is the objective, the short-f lens the eyepiece, and the far target is a window scene at the end of the bench.

The one equation

  • 1/o + 1/i = 1/f — the thin-lens equation. All distances in metres.
  • m = −i/o — the magnification. The size is |m|; a minus means inverted. This formula is added; the manual does not print it.
  • spacing = fo + fe and M = fo/fe for the refractor. The magnification formula is also added.

What the screen shows

A real image lands on the white screen, inverted, at the size m says.

A virtual image never lands on the screen. You see it only by looking through the lens, and it is erect and larger.

Sharp focus is a band a few millimetres wide, not one exact mark.

The honest limits

The thin-lens equation ignores lens thickness and lens errors.

Sharp focus spans about 3 to 5 mm on a real bench. This bench grades inside a 4 mm band, and never marks one perfect spot.

Two lens positions exist only when the arrow and screen stand at least 4f apart. The bench says so instead of hiding it.

The refractor part has no measurement sheet in the manual. You record the alignment in words only.

Common mistakes in this lab:
  • Logging a position where the image is still soft.
  • Reading o from the screen mark instead of the lens mark.
  • Hunting for the 0.5f image on the screen. It is virtual.
  • Swapping the lenses in the refractor and expecting the same magnification.

Part 1 — Focal lengths from two positions

  1. Pick "Lens A" or "Lens B". That lens goes in the holder.
  2. Turn the light on. The arrow is lit.
  3. Drag the lens along the bench, between the arrow and the screen.
  4. Stop when the arrow is sharp on the screen. The bench note tells you.
  5. Press "Log position 1". The bench reads o and i off the marks.
  6. Keep dragging. The image sharpens again at a second spot.
  7. Press "Log position 2". Both rows of the first table fill.

Part 2 — Four printed object distances

  1. Pick the lens, A or B, if you have not already.
  2. Press "2.5f", "2.0f", "1.5f" or "0.5f" to place the arrow.
  3. Drag the screen until the arrow is sharp. Read o and i off the marks.
  4. Pick the size, the up-or-down sense, and the type from what you see.
  5. Press "Log row". The row lands in the second table.
  6. At 0.5f nothing lands on the screen. That is the lesson. Log it as virtual.

Part 3 — The two-lens refractor

  1. Pick which lens is the objective. The long-f lens gives the bigger view.
  2. The other lens becomes the eyepiece on its own holder.
  3. The window scene at the bench end is the far target. It is always in.
  4. Drag the eyepiece until the window is sharp in the viewport.
  5. The spacing readout should sit near fo + fe, and the view is inverted.
  6. Press "Record in words". A sentence lands in your log.

The sim’s own setup. The bench photo is image-only, so this 100 cm rail, the 1 mm scale, the arrow post and the window scene are the sim’s own standard build. The sharpness band is ±4 mm, inside the real bench’s ±3–5 mm.

Fun Mode noise. Each o and i reading carries a stable ±2 mm scale-reading offset, the way a real eye finds a mark. Fun Mode never changes the focal lengths, the printed distances, or the accepted values.
