Lab 6 · Torque balance on a meter stick

Hang masses on a meter stick until the counter-clockwise and clockwise torques match, then weigh an unknown object against a known one. The bench grades with the clamps included, and a stick that reads 1–3 % off still counts as level.

Words in this lab

  • torque — the turning effect of a pull. Written τ = F·d, in newton-metres.
  • lever arm — the distance d from the pivot to the line of the pull, in metres.
  • equilibrium — the state where the counter-clockwise torque equals the clockwise torque.
  • balance point X₀ — the mark where the stick sits level with nothing hanging.
  • clamp-and-hanger — one clamp and its hanger at one mark. The pair’s mass counts on that side.
  • triple-beam balance — the scale that weighs the clamp and the unknown object.
  • newton-metre — the unit of torque. A pull of one newton, one metre from the pivot.

What you do at the bench

The bench has a meter stick on a pivot clamp. It has three clamp-and-hanger sets, hook weights of 100 g and 50 g, an unknown object, and a triple-beam balance.

In Part I you hang 100 g at the 30 cm mark on the left. Then you move the right clamp until the stick reads level with the 50 g on it. The marks give you X2 and both lever arms.

In Part II the unknown object takes the left hook. You weigh it on the triple-beam balance, then weigh it again by balancing it against the 100 g. The two numbers should agree.

In Part III a third clamp joins the pair. You choose its mark and its extra weight, and the bench checks all three torques at once.

The two equations

A pull at a distance turns the stick. The size of the turn is the torque:

  • τ = F·d

The stick stays level when the two turns match, one pulling each way:

  • τccw = τcw

With hanging masses, g cancels in the balance line, so you can carry it in grams: M1·d1 = M2·d2. The bench works in gram-metres and prints newton-metres.

The unknown object rides the same line, with a named form of its own:

  • Mu = (Mk + Mc)·d2/d1 − Mc

All the masses sit in grams, because g cancels out of the balance line.

Why the clamps count

Each clamp-and-hanger sits at its own mark and pulls its own side down. Ignore the 40 g and the balance mark shifts by centimetres.

Weigh the clamp first on the triple-beam balance. Then keep it in every torque the bench checks, or the balance mark lands in the wrong place.

Level, in the honest sense

A real stick balances near 50 cm, rarely exactly at it. The drawer shows the stick’s own X₀, and Fun Mode lets you drag the pivot to find it.

Your eye judges level within a millimetre or two. The bench reads the same way: a 1–3 % gap between the torques still counts as level.

Common mistakes in this lab:
  • Reading d from the zero end of the stick instead of from the pivot.
  • Dropping the clamp-and-hanger mass from the totals.
  • Mixing cm and m in the torque line.
  • Trusting the first level you see, before the stick has settled.
  • Weighing the unknown with the clamp still on it, then using the hook number.

Part I — Balance 100 g against 50 g

  1. Weigh the clamp and hanger on the triple-beam balance. The drawer says 40 g, and the scale should agree.
  2. Set the left clamp at X1 = 0.30 m and hang the 100 g hook weight on it.
  3. Hang the right clamp with its 50 g weight somewhere past the pivot.
  4. Use the fine-adjust slider to move X2 until the stick reads level.
  5. Press "Log Part I". The row writes M1T, M2T, X1, X2, F1, F2, d1, d2 and both torques.
  6. The footer gives the percent difference of the two torques. A few tenths of a percent is a good bench.

Part II — Weigh the unknown

  1. Take the 100 g off the left hook and hang the unknown object in its place.
  2. Keep the left clamp at X1 = 0.30 m, so d1 = 0.20 m.
  3. Move the right clamp to X2 = 0.75 m, so d2 = 0.25 m, with the 100 g hook weight on it.
  4. Nudge the fine-adjust slider until the stick reads level.
  5. Press "Log Part II". The bench computes M_u from your marks, with the clamp mass included.
  6. Weigh the unknown on the triple-beam balance. The footer compares the measured and the computed mass in percent.

Part III — Three clamps, one balance

  1. The left and right clamps keep their Part I loads: 100 g at 0.20 m and 50 g at 0.25 m from the pivot.
  2. Drop the third clamp on the right side and slide it to the mark you choose, at d3 from the pivot.
  3. Add 10 g, 20 g and 50 g weights to the third hanger until the stick reads level with all three clamps.
  4. Press "Log Part III". The bench writes the condition out: each torque, and what the third clamp must supply.
  5. The footer gives both sides of the condition and the percent difference once all three are on.
  6. At d3 = 0.10 m the answer is 15 g added. Your mark may differ, and the condition must still close.

Drag this — the clamps slide along the stick, and the pivot itself moves in Fun Mode.

The stick tilts when the torques disagree. When the tilt reads as level, the bench says so out loud.
