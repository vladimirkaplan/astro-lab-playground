Lab 1 · Measurement

Rulers, balances, and two unit systems. Behind every measurement sits one honest question: how far is your number from the accepted one?

Words in this lab

  • parallax — the wrong reading you get when you view a scale from an angle.
  • zero mark — the 0 line on a ruler. Start every measure there.
  • accepted value — the value that scientists agree to use.
  • percent error — how far your value is from the accepted value, as a percent.
  • mass — how much matter an object holds. It is the same everywhere.
  • weight — the pull of gravity on that mass. It changes with place.
  • balance — a tool that compares weights to find mass.

What this lab is about

The first lab is not really about the table or the coins. It is about how to measure anything and report it honestly. You will measure one object twice. First use a meter stick in centimeters. Then use a yard stick in inches. Divide the two answers. You will find the centimeters-per-inch factor with your own hands, not from a formula sheet.

You will also weigh coins and switch between two unit systems. And you will learn one habit you can reuse all year: percent error. It compares your result to the accepted value. The accepted value is the value scientists agree to use.

The physics of a good reading

A ruler only gives what its ticks can show, plus one guessed digit. A meter stick has millimeter ticks. So read it to the nearest tenth of a centimeter. Do not claim more.

Two mistakes can ruin a reading before you do any math. The first mistake is parallax. Parallax means you read the ruler from an angle. The object edge then lines up with the wrong mark. A big gap between object and ruler makes the error worse. The second mistake is alignment. Always start at the zero mark. Do not start at the end of the ruler. If you start in the middle, subtract the starting mark.

One reading is never the last word. Repeat it. The repeats will scatter a little. That scatter is random error. A badly zeroed tool is worse. It shifts every reading the same way. That shift is systematic error.

Percent error boils this down to one number. Take your measured value minus the accepted value. Ignore the sign if the answer comes out negative. Divide by the accepted value, then times 100. Because the sign is ignored, an early high reading and a late low one score the same.

Mass and weight are not the same thing. Mass counts the matter in an object. It is the same everywhere. Weight is the gravitational force on that matter. It changes with location. A balance compares the object's weight with the weight of known masses. So a balance reads mass correctly anywhere, even on the Moon.

Why this matters to astronomers: astronomers can measure almost nothing directly. Every distance, mass, temperature, or amount in the field is an inference. Each small number carries its own percent error. Those errors multiply through the whole calculation. Care with units is not busywork. In 1999 a Mars orbiter crashed because a team mixed English and metric numbers. (Grounded in OpenStax Astronomy 2e, Ch. 1-2, CC BY 4.0.)

Common mistakes in this lab:
  • Measuring from the end of the ruler, not from the zero mark.
  • Reading the scale from the side, not straight above (parallax).
  • Writing digits finer than the ruler can show.
  • Rounding before the final step.
  • Flipping the conversion factor and getting 0.394 cm per inch.
  • Copying the accepted value into your table as your own reading.
  • Reporting a signed percent error. This course uses the size only.

Part A: the lab bench

  1. Pick Instrument: Ideal (exact) or Fun (noisy) at the top of the tab.
  2. Read the scale mark under the bench edge with the Eye position slider at 0.
  3. Slide Eye position to +100, read again, and see parallax change the number.
  4. Return the Eye position slider to 0, so your eye sits straight above the scale.
  5. Drag the marker at the far end of the bench until it sits on the edge.
  6. Press "Switch to inches ruler" and pair the inch reading with the centimeter reading.
  7. Press "Log this reading".
  8. Press "Slide ruler off zero", subtract the start mark, and log the fixed reading.

Part B: the coins

  1. Click a coin in the tray to pick it.
  2. Press "Weigh on the balance" and read the mass.
  3. Press "Show specs" and compare the reading with the US Mint value.
  4. Switch Instrument to Fun and weigh the same coin three times.
  5. Press "New pair" in the percent-error trainer.
  6. Work out the percent error and type it into the box.
  7. Press "Check" and read the feedback.
  8. Repeat with three more pairs until the formula feels boring.
